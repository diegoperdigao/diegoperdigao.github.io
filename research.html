Key Research Areas

Federated Learning & Edge Intelligence

Federated Learning (FL) has emerged as an essential paradigm for training machine learning models collaboratively across decentralized edge clients without transmitting raw, sensitive sensory measurements to a central server. In our research, we explore federated optimization under real-world IoT challenges: non-IID (non-identically and independently distributed) data, client drift, asynchronous client updates, and tight computational and bandwidth constraints. We develop novel federated frameworks that achieve robust convergence while preserving privacy and minimizing communication overhead.

Prototype-Based Classifiers & Interpretable Machine Learning

Unlike high-capacity black-box models whose internal reasoning remains opaque, prototype-based classifiers—such as Learning Vector Quantization (LVQ) and its metric-learning extensions (GLVQ, GRLVQ, GMLVQ)—represent class knowledge explicitly as intuitive, human-inspectable reference prototypes in the input feature space. Our research investigates prototype-based architectures capable of yielding high classification accuracy, robust rejection thresholds for out-of-distribution events, and interpretable decision mechanisms suitable for mission-critical engineering applications.

Smart Water Networks & Leakage Anomaly Detection

Municipal water distribution networks (WDNs) are vital urban infrastructures subject to significant losses due to physical pipe bursts and undetected background leaks. We apply advanced statistical signal processing and machine learning techniques to high-resolution hydraulic measurements (pressure transients, flow rates, and acoustic sensor signals). By integrating federated prototype models directly into edge telemetry loggers, we enable early detection and localization of real-world water leakage events while respecting data sovereignty across district metered areas (DMAs).

Predictive Maintenance & Fault Diagnosis in Electric Machinery

Condition monitoring of three-phase induction motors is critical for preventing catastrophic failures in manufacturing and energy facilities. Our work focuses on non-invasive diagnosis of early-stage stator winding inter-turn short-circuit faults. We explore acoustic emission signals, vibration signatures, and motor current signature analysis (MCSA) combined with prototype classifiers to achieve reliable fault discrimination across varying load regimes.

Selected Research Projects

Federated Prototype-Based Intelligence for Water Distribution Infrastructures

Description: Development of decentralized, privacy-preserving machine learning frameworks deployed on IoT sensor nodes for monitoring municipal water distribution networks. Focused on localized model training, prototype synchronization, and robust anomaly classification for real-world leak detection.
Partners: Federal University of Ceará (UFC, Brazil) and KTH Royal Institute of Technology (Sweden).

Intelligent Condition Monitoring and Fault Diagnosis in Induction Motors

Description: Investigation of metric-learning and prototype-based classifiers for non-invasive short-circuit fault identification in three-phase induction motors, evaluating multi-sensor feature extraction (acoustic and electrical) under operational variations.
Affiliation: Department of Teleinformatics Engineering (DETI), UFC.
